The Opportunity Is Often Already There

Businesses naturally focus on what is happening today.

New leads. New opportunities. Current customers. Current projects.

Meanwhile, valuable relationships quietly age inside the CRM.

Those relationships do not necessarily disappear because the opportunity is gone.

Sometimes they disappear because nobody owns the follow-up.

  • A customer who bought from you three years ago may still need what you sell.
  • A prospect who said “not now” may be ready today.
  • A decision maker may have changed.
  • An old estimate may never have received a real follow-up.
  • A referral partner may simply have stopped hearing from you.

The 5x5 Approach

Five Purposeful Touches, Paced to Your Sales Cycle

5x5 is not five channels. It is not five automated emails. And it is not a harassment campaign.

It is a structured follow-up process built around five purposeful touches across calls, email, mail, and LinkedIn. For a short sales cycle, that can take about five weeks. For complex, high-value sales, it often runs two months or more. Here is one typical sequence:

  1. Week 1 · Email

    Start with a useful, relevant reason to reconnect.

  2. Week 2 · Phone

    Follow the email with a personal call.

  3. Week 3 · Direct Mail

    Mail makes the next email or call more recognizable.

  4. Week 4 · Email

    Another relevant reason to respond, building on the last touch.

  5. Week 5 · Phone

    Speak directly with the contact and decide what happens next.

Mail can be a letter, a postcard, or a package, depending on the account.

A channel can be used more than once. The objective is not to check five boxes. The objective is to create enough useful, human contact to restart a conversation.

The Sequence Should Match the Relationship

5x5 is a framework. It is not a rigid script.

A former customer should not necessarily receive the same sequence as a dormant B2B account. A referral partner should not receive the same message as someone with an old estimate.

The audience determines:

  • The message
  • The timing
  • The offer
  • The tone
  • The channel mix
  • The desired next step

We use the 5x5 framework to create consistency without making the communication feel mechanical.
