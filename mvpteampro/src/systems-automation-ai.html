Give Your Team Better Information Before They Act

Automation does not always mean doing something automatically. Sometimes it means helping someone make a better decision.

For example, a salesperson preparing to approach a potential referral partner can use a system we built. Instead of spending time manually researching every company, the salesperson starts the conversation better prepared.

  1. Enter a company website
  2. Review the business and summarize what it does
  3. Compare it against the ideal partner profile
  4. Provide a potential fit score
  5. Identify useful conversation points

Better information. Better decisions. Better conversations.

Remove the Steps That Slow Everything Down

A lot of good ideas never happen because there are simply too many steps involved. Eventually, people stop doing it.

We build systems that reduce that friction. For example, a team member can submit information through something as simple as an email. From there, automation and AI can help organize the information, apply the appropriate brand voice, prepare it for different channels, and move it into the marketing workflow.

The easier something is to do, the more likely it is to actually get done.

  • Someone takes a great photo inside the business.
  • Then someone has to write the post.
  • Someone has to make sure it sounds right.
  • Someone has to format it.
  • Someone has to resize or prepare the content.
  • Someone has to load it into a marketing system.
  • Someone has to publish it.

We Keep Improving What We Build

One of the biggest differences between MVP and a traditional technology project is what happens after the system launches. We keep using it. We see where another manual step has appeared, and where the information could be better. And we keep improving the process.

  1. Operate

    Our team runs the system every day.

  2. Learn

    We see what works and where someone still gets stuck.

  3. Improve

    We fix the steps that slow things down.

  4. Automate

    We let the system handle what it can.

  5. Operate Better

    And the cycle starts again.

We are not trying to build perfect technology on day one. We are trying to build a better way to get the work done, and then keep making it better.

Sometimes You Don’t Need to Start Over

The same thinking applies to larger systems. Sometimes an existing process, website, or technology foundation is still valuable. It simply needs to be modernized.

In one project, an existing print-based design was turned into a reusable CMS framework. The system was continually improved while content was created using a deeply developed business persona and structured workflow.

The result was a website of more than 800 pages built from a system designed to make ongoing expansion easier.

We call this kind of approach a forklift upgrade.

  • Keep what works.
  • Replace what doesn’t.
  • Improve the underlying system.
  • Then keep moving.
