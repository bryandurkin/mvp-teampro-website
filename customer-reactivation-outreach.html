The Opportunity Is Often Already There

Businesses naturally focus on what is happening today.

New leads. New opportunities. Current customers. Current projects.

Meanwhile, valuable relationships quietly age inside the CRM.

A customer who bought from you three years ago may still need what you sell.
A prospect who said “not now” may be ready today.
A decision maker may have changed.
An old estimate may never have received a real follow-up.
A referral partner may simply have stopped hearing from you.

Those relationships do not necessarily disappear because the opportunity is gone.

Sometimes they disappear because nobody owns the follow-up.

Your Database Is Getting Older Every Day

In the B2B and industrial databases we have worked with, we regularly see more than 21% of contact records change over time.

That is why reactivation is not simply about sending another email. It starts with understanding who is actually inside the database.

Customer information does not stay accurate forever.

  • People change jobs.
  • Companies move.
  • Email addresses change.
  • Phone numbers change.
  • Decision makers leave.
  • Customers change roles.

And sometimes the information is technically correct, but the relationship itself has simply gone cold.

There Is More Than One Kind of Reactivation Opportunity

Past Customers

Customers who already know your business are often easier to reconnect with than people who have never heard of you.

Reactivation can help bring them back for:

  • repeat services
  • maintenance
  • additional products
  • seasonal needs
  • upgrades
  • new projects

Dormant Accounts

Some customer relationships stop without anyone really deciding they are over.

The account simply goes quiet.

A thoughtful check-in can help determine:

  • whether there is still an opportunity
  • whether the contact person changed
  • whether the customer moved to another provider
  • whether timing simply changed

Even when the answer is no, the CRM becomes more accurate.

Old Estimates & Opportunities

An estimate that did not close six months ago is not necessarily worthless today.

The customer’s circumstances may have changed. The timing may now be better. The original objection may no longer exist. Or they may have simply gotten busy and never made a decision.

We can create a structured process for revisiting those opportunities instead of leaving them permanently open or forgotten.

Former Prospects

Not every prospect becomes a customer the first time you speak with them.

Sometimes the fit was right and the timing was wrong.

Reactivation gives you a reason to reconnect without pretending the previous conversation never happened.

Referral Partners

Referral relationships need attention too.

A good referral partner can forget about your company just as easily as a customer can.

Periodic, useful outreach helps keep those relationships active and keeps your company easier to remember when an opportunity appears.

Annual & Seasonal Customers

Some businesses have natural timing built into the customer relationship.

  • Annual service.
  • Seasonal maintenance.
  • Inspections.
  • Renewals.
  • Recurring purchases.

Follow-up should happen before the customer has to remember to call you.

The 5×5 Approach

Five Purposeful Touches Over Five Weeks

5×5 is not five channels. It is not five automated emails. And it is not a five-week harassment campaign.

It is a structured follow-up process built around five purposeful touches over five weeks using the right combination of: Phone · Email · Direct Mail

  1. Week 1

    Email

  2. Week 2

    Phone

  3. Week 3

    Direct Mail

  4. Week 4

    Email

  5. Week 5

    Phone & Final Follow-Up

A channel can be used more than once. The objective is not to check five boxes. The objective is to create enough useful, human contact to restart a conversation.

What a 5×5 Campaign Can Look Like

Week 1: Email

Start with a useful, relevant reason to reconnect.

That might be:

  • a simple check-in
  • a new service
  • an annual reminder
  • a useful resource
  • a change in the business
  • an opportunity worth revisiting

The goal is not to force a sale. It is to reopen the conversation.

Week 2: Phone

Follow the email with a personal call.

Depending on the relationship, that might involve:

  • confirming the right contact
  • checking whether the timing has changed
  • identifying current needs
  • leaving a useful voicemail
  • updating the CRM

Phone creates a level of human connection that automated outreach cannot.

Week 3: Direct Mail

Physical mail creates a different kind of touch.

Depending on the campaign, that might include:

  • a postcard
  • a letter
  • a handwritten-style note
  • a printed resource
  • a book
  • another relevant physical piece

Direct mail does not have to carry the entire sales message. Sometimes its job is simply to make the next email or phone call more recognizable.

Week 4: Email

Now we follow up with another relevant reason to respond.

The message may reference:

  • the previous conversation
  • the mailed piece
  • a customer problem
  • useful information
  • a specific opportunity
  • a clear next step

The sequence should feel coordinated rather than repetitive.

Week 5: Phone & Final Follow-Up

The final touch gives us another opportunity to speak directly with the contact and determine what happens next.

That might mean:

  • scheduling a conversation
  • updating the contact record
  • identifying a new decision maker
  • setting a later follow-up date
  • documenting that there is no current opportunity
  • moving the relationship into another ongoing communication program

A campaign can create value even when someone does not buy immediately. We learn more about the relationship.

The Sequence Should Match the Relationship

5×5 is a framework. It is not a rigid script.

A former customer should not necessarily receive the same sequence as a dormant B2B account. A referral partner should not receive the same message as someone with an old estimate.

The audience determines:

  • the message
  • the timing
  • the offer
  • the tone
  • the channel mix
  • the desired next step

We use the 5×5 framework to create consistency without making the communication feel mechanical.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 5×5 five different marketing channels?

No.

5×5 means five purposeful touches over five weeks.

We typically use some combination of phone, email, and direct mail. The same channel may appear more than once when that makes sense.

Does every campaign use the same sequence?

No.

The 5×5 framework creates consistency, but the actual sequence should reflect the audience, relationship, campaign objective, and available customer information.

Do you clean the database before starting?

We can.

Database review and contact validation are often part of the work, especially when the list has not been actively maintained.

We also continue improving the data as outreach occurs.

Is this only for past customers?

No.

Campaigns can include:

  • past customers
  • dormant accounts
  • former prospects
  • old estimates
  • referral partners
  • decision makers
  • annual-service customers
  • other existing relationships
Do you write the outreach?

Yes.

We can help build the messaging and sequence around the campaign objective, audience, and existing relationship.

Can you make the phone calls too?

Yes.

Human phone outreach is an important part of many 5×5 campaigns.

Do you handle direct mail?

We can help coordinate the direct-mail portion of a campaign, including the list, messaging, timing, and follow-up around the piece.

Can you work inside our CRM?

Yes, whenever practical.

We prefer to document the activity directly in the systems your team already uses.

What happens when someone responds?

The process is defined before the campaign begins.

Depending on the response, our team may:

  • schedule a meeting
  • update the record
  • create a follow-up
  • transfer the opportunity
  • escalate the conversation to your internal sales team
Can this become an ongoing program?

Yes.

Some companies begin with a defined reactivation project and then move into ongoing:
